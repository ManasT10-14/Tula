# Legal source and machine-check matrix

Research snapshot: **7 September 2026**. This document audits the 18 rule files in `rules/lmpcr-2011`, their interpreter and Python exemption filter. The baseline inspected was pack `2026.09.07-draft`. The supplied SIH artifact and the user's complete 43-section objective define the product requirements; they are **not statutory authority**. This bounded audit does not reduce that 43-section scope, certify the product, or claim a complete consolidation of Indian law.

The machine-check descriptions below describe that baseline. Implementation follow-up must record a new pack version, retain the baseline archive, and test both the correction and its applicability boundary. Passing one label screen must never be presented as certification of the package or proof of compliance with every applicable law.

## Implemented screening update

The subsequent implementation uses **`2026.09.07-legal-review-1`**, retaining all 18 stable rule IDs and the original serialized pack in `rules/lmpcr-2011/archive/2026.09.07-draft.json`. Each current rule records its primary source metadata. The unchanged archive retains its original semantics when explicitly selected; it is not retrospectively “corrected” while keeping its old version label.

Implemented changes include the Rule 6(2) contact citation and dated component requirements; prescribed unit-price denominators and rounded Decimal arithmetic; scoped combination/group/multipiece relief; review of alcoholic-liquor and exact kg/litre/metre boundaries; qualified generic manufacture-date screening; imported-status confirmation and the 2018 origin commencement; competing-MRP review; the corrected 2.0 mm blown/formed cell; dated tobacco/pan-masala carve-outs; medical-device referral; historical rounding through the unambiguous period followed by transition review; and effective-dated enforcement references.

OCR contact omissions remain inconclusive until verified transcription/coverage can establish absence. Confirmed ordinary single-package contexts can receive deterministic unit-price findings; unresolved category/package facts gate only the dependent decisions. Measurements are preserved during legal review without inventing a threshold. Exemption clues detected outside normalized declarations remain available to subsequent evaluations. Metadata import rejects unknown keys, invalid dates, unsupported decision kinds and arbitrary regular expressions.

This update does **not** implement a complete FSSAI/MDR/State-excise code, certify postal addresses or contact reachability, resolve the full early-adoption/GST/2026 e-commerce transition history, establish registry facts from images, or automate notice authority. The matrix below remains the baseline audit and identifies those continuing gaps. New source-boundary regressions are in `tests/test_legal_versions.py`; existing engine fixtures now explicitly supply their known category, package structure and geometry.

## Source method and limits

Sources were discovered through the [Department of Consumer Affairs legal-metrology index][S0], checked against the actual official PDFs, and cross-checked with the earlier `docs/AUDIT.md` sources. Gazette amendments take precedence over FAQs and state compilations. Several official PDFs were retrieved with Windows' certificate-validating HTTP client when browser retrieval timed out. Scanned tables/contact amendments were rendered and visually checked. The Department's linked consolidated e-book at `https://doca.gov.in/lm-ebook/` could not be retrieved reliably; its certificate was expired during this audit. That is a retrieval limit, not evidence that the underlying law has ceased to apply.

Publication date, commencement date, date of manufacture, packing date, import date, sale date and listing date are different facts. Historical transitions, category-specific orders, State excise rules and other enactments require legal review where the sources below do not resolve them. A notification listed on a website is not necessarily already in force. This document deliberately identifies unresolved transitions instead of inventing an effective date.

## Priority conclusions

1. **Small blown/formed packages: keep 2.0 mm.** The 2017 Table I initially printed 1.5 mm in its first blown/formed cell. The November corrigendum changes that cell to 2.0 mm. This resolves the discrepancy noted in the old audit; the FAQ and corrected Gazette agree. [2017 amendment, English p.11][S4]; [corrigendum, English p.2][S5].
2. **Consumer care is Rule 6(2), not Rule 6(1)(f).** The 2015 replacement, effective 1 January 2016, requires the contact person's/office's name, address, telephone number and e-mail address. The existing `phone OR email` predicate is incomplete. An OCR contact-block parse still cannot prove that a postal address is complete or that a contact is reachable. [G.S.R.385(E), English p.4][S3].
3. **Unit-price arithmetic needs a statutory denominator.** A numerically correct price per 100 g on a 200 g package is not a substitute for the prescribed per-gram basis. Current exception checks must include the category and package structure. The replacement text uses `<`/`>` around 1 kg, 1 litre and 1 metre; exact equality needs the equal-MRP exception or manual interpretation, not an invented boundary. [G.S.R.226(E), English pp.2–3][S7].
4. **Food dates require a separate legal route.** Food manufacture/packing and expiry/use-by duties have exceptions and do not match the generic PCR date predicate. Their interpretation also depends on shelf life, surface area and outer-package evidence. [FSSAI compendium, regulations 5(10), 8(1)(a), 8(3), pp.16–17, 21–22][S18].
5. **Multiple MRP values do not establish an offence.** Lower-price stickers and time-bound GST relaxations can require the original MRP to remain visible. Review the relationship between prices, label layers, dates and the actual sale. [PCR Rule 6(3), official state compilation][S16]; [DoCA's 18 September 2025 order][S21].
6. **Current notices cannot use old penalty assumptions.** The 2026 amendment substitutes section 36(1), including an improvement notice for the first offence and different later-offence sanctions. It commenced on 1 May 2026. Automated label findings do not establish offender identity, offence history or the officer's statutory decision. [Jan Vishwas Act 2026, Schedule item 66(N), pp.147–148][S19]; [S.O.2103(E)][S20].

## Versioning and acceptance requirements

- Store the complete resolved pack JSON and content hash under its immutable version before changing rules. Preserve original findings and evidence in historical inspection revisions. A rescan uses a new version and explicitly links its predecessor.
- Attach primary source URLs, notification numbers, publication/commencement dates, affected clauses, review status and precise supported time interval to each rule. Distinguish a screening paraphrase from a verbatim quotation.
- Represent legal applicability using confirmed facts plus uncertainty, not keyword-based global exemptions. Missing category, date, shape or bundle facts should block only the decisions that depend on them.
- Regression cases should include pre/post-2016 care contacts, 2018 corrected height bands, 2024 unit-price bases and exceptions, lawful revised-MRP labels, food shelf-life/outer-pack dates, 2026 pan masala, medical-device referral, ambiguous exact quantity boundaries and historical rounding.
- Legal acceptance still needs an authorised legal review of the complete amendment/order history and applicable non-PCR regimes. Image smoke tests cannot supply that review. The wider 43-section requirements for acquisition, security, reports, review, product intelligence, deployment and field validation remain separate acceptance work.
